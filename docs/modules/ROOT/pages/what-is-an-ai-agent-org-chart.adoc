= What Is an AI Agent Org Chart?

[.lead]
_Draw the roles first, then write in the people and the agents_

[.in-short]
*In short:* An AI agent org chart is a chart of the roles an organisation needs, each with a one-sentence outcome line and a named human owner, with people and agents written in as the current occupants. In Gareth Price's _The Blueprint_, the boxes are roles, not bots, and the chart is drawn for the company you are becoming.

== A Chart of Roles, With Occupants Written In

Each box is a role: a position defined by the result it produces, with an outcome line beginning "To…", standards, a charter and one human owner. An occupant fills it: a person, an agent, both, or, early on, the founder.

Three rules keep the chart useful:

* *Roles, not bots.* One agent can hold several roles, each with its own charter. How many agents fill the chart is a separate decision you can change later without redrawing it.
* *One boss per role, one writer per piece of work.* Every role reports to exactly one boss, only one role changes any given piece of work, and every chain ends in a human who owns the outcome.
* *Small spans.* Three to five reports is a sensible start for an agent manager. Count the human who reviews the agents' work too, because that span usually breaks first.

Size it for the company you are becoming, perhaps three times your current scale, so it shows the roles you will need.

== Why It Matters: Most Agent Org Charts Are Accidents

Most agent org charts are drawn after the fact, as a list of whatever got built. The author's own workstation had fourteen agent profiles. Nearly all reported to one person. One box had no occupant and nobody had removed it. One agent had no identity at all. Two agents both triaged tickets. The chart was never drawn; it was accumulated.

With people, an accidental chart takes years to collect. With agents it takes a fortnight. A designed chart separates the job from the occupant, so the box survives a new model or a resignation. It shows the gaps, the roles nobody fills. And it shows duplicates: two QA agents look like initiative on a list, but on a chart they are two occupants in one box, and someone must choose.

== Example: Customer Success at a Small Software Company

xref:appendix-c-worked-example.adoc[Appendix C] follows a fictional 26-person software company that drew its customer success chart before filling it. Two agents hold five roles between them:

[cols="2,3,2",options="header"]
|===
|Role |Outcome line (shortened) |Occupant

|Account Owner
|To own each customer's relationship and results
|Two people

|Customer Correspondent
|To give customers accurate, clear answers within two working hours
|A general agent

|Account Health Monitor
|To spot customers at risk early enough to help
|The same general agent

|Draft Reviewer
|To check every customer-bound draft before a person sees it
|A separate agent, so the checker never shares the context of what it checks

|Renewal Preparer
|To prepare each renewal conversation sixty days ahead
|Vacant: Account Owners cover for now
|===

One draft role failed the role coherence test: a "Customer Agent" that answered questions and also offered credits for outages. Answers are routine and reversible; credits are money. Credits moved to the Account Owner.

== How to Draw One This Week

. *List the results* the organisation must produce at your chosen size: invoices sent, customers onboarded, releases tested. Results, not tasks.
. *Group them into functions*, then turn each group into roles with an outcome line starting "To…".
. *Name a human owner for every role*, especially the ones an agent will fill.
. *Write in today's occupant:* a person, an agent, or you.
. *Mark the gaps and duplicates.* Ask Claude, ChatGPT or Grok to interview you one question at a time and produce the table, then list every empty role, every role with two occupants and every role you hold yourself.

== How It Differs From an Agent Inventory or a Workflow Diagram

An agent inventory lists what exists, accidents included; nothing on it says what the organisation needs. A workflow diagram shows how work moves step by step between tools; that is the how, which belongs in a role's playbooks. An AI agent org chart answers different questions: which results the organisation needs, which role owns each one, who that role reports to, and which human answers for it. Build the inventory, then use it to fill in the chart.
